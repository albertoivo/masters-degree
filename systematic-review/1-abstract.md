# Abstract

Equatorial Plasma Bubbles (EPBs) cause severe ionospheric scintillations that disrupt satellite communication and navigation systems. Because manual detection is subjective and unscalable, automated monitoring is critical. This systematic review investigates the integration of Machine Learning (ML) techniques to automate the detection, classification, and feature extraction of EPBs. Following the PRISMA protocol, 11 relevant studies published between 2022 and 2025 were analyzed. The literature reveals a dominant reliance on All-Sky Imager optical data and Very High Frequency radar imagery. Deep Convolutional Neural Networks and hybrid ML architectures constitute the primary methodological trends. These models consistently achieve detection accuracies exceeding 90%, with optimized ensemble and Explainable AI frameworks reaching over 99% while drastically reducing computational costs. Furthermore, automated morphological segmentation has enabled unprecedented solar-cycle-scale statistical profiling of EPB characteristics. Ultimately, ML represents a transformative paradigm in space weather monitoring, offering robust, real-time operational capabilities. Future research must address prevailing limitations, specifically dataset imbalances and susceptibility to adverse weather conditions, by advancing multi-instrument data fusion and predictive spatiotemporal modeling.
